{\large Exact reachable sizes $1$ through $20$}\par\vspace{5mm}
A seed has a distinguished root, two outgoing ports at every vertex, and unrestricted indegree. Loops and coincident ports are allowed. Only root-accessibility is required.\par\vspace{2mm}
Every mask is static: $\delta_\sigma(s,x)=\tau_{x\mathbin{\oplus}\sigma(s)}(s)$. The score $H(G)$ is the shortest positive length of two distinct, equal-length words whose endpoint vectors agree for all masks.\par\vspace{2mm}
Let $F(m)=\max_{|G|=m} H(G)$, where exactly $m$ states are reachable. Root-preserving relabeling and independent port swaps identify graph classes. Different roots can therefore give different classes of the same ordinary shape.\par\vspace{4mm}

{\Large\bfseries Graph edits and extremal growth}\par\vspace{4mm}
We work on rooted isomorphism classes: vertex relabeling and independent port swaps cost zero. A port retarget changes one outgoing target and costs one. A routing insertion also costs one: choose two distinct edge occurrences $a\to b$ and $c\to d$, add a nonroot vertex $v$, and replace them by\[a\to v,\qquad c\to v,\qquad v\to b,\qquad v\to d.\]Sources or targets may coincide; edge occurrences remain distinct. Its inverse removes a nonroot vertex of indegree two without a loop and reconnects its incoming occurrences to its outgoing targets in either bijection.\par\vspace{2mm}
The loop version replaces $a\to b$ by $a\to v$, $v\to v$, $v\to b$; its inverse smooths that nonroot vertex. Every intermediate seed must remain root-accessible and have two outgoing ports. The root is never deleted. The distance is the minimum number of these edits; this is a chosen unit cost for a growth operation, rather than a count of changed arcs.\par\vspace{3mm}
\textbf{Consecutive sizes.} The table exhausts all allowed one-vertex smoothings of every certified maximizing child. It does not assume that a maximizing child must have a maximizing parent.\par\vspace{3mm}
\begin{center}\small\begin{tabular}{crrrll}
\toprule Sizes & Parent $F$ & Child $F$ & Winners & One insertion & Edit distances \\ \midrule
$1\to2$ & 1 & 3 & 1 & 1/1 & $1$ \\
$2\to3$ & 3 & 6 & 2 & 0/2 & $2,2$ \\
$3\to4$ & 6 & 10 & 1 & 0/1 & $2$ \\
$4\to5$ & 10 & 15 & 5 & 0/5 & $3,3,3,2,2$ \\
$5\to6$ & 15 & 23 & 2 & 1/2 & $1,2$ \\
$6\to7$ & 23 & 34 & 8 & 1/8 & $3,3,3,1,3,3,3,3$ \\
\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}\vspace{3mm}
Edit distances are listed in child panel order. Each is the minimum distance to the entire previous-size winning collection. Complete unit neighborhoods exclude shorter paths, and explicit saved paths attain every displayed distance. The paths may pass through graphs that are not maximizers.\par\vspace{2mm}
For $5\to6$, five-state panels (a) and (b) both grow into six-state panel (a), raising $H$ from $15$ to $23$. From panel (b), split $0\to1$ and $2\to3$ through the new vertex $5$, with outgoing targets $\{1,3\}$. All five previous winners are included in the comparison; panels (c), (d), and (e) have no winning child by this single insertion.\par\vspace{2mm}
Six-state panel (b) has best predecessor score $14$, below $F(5)=15$. One such predecessor has the same ordinary shape as a five-state winner but a different root. Retaining only rooted winning parents would already omit a winning child.\par\vspace{2mm}
With retargeting allowed, six-state panel (b) is at distance two from the five-state winning collection: one retarget followed by one insertion suffices. Thus every winning child through seven states lies within three edits of some previous-size winner under this metric.\par\vspace{2mm}
The six-state winner reached from five-state winners is panel (a), whereas the six-state winner leading to a seven-state winner is panel (b). Hence there is no chain of maximizing rooted graphs $5\to6\to7$ using only one such insertion at each step.\par\vspace{2mm}
The edits preserve old indegrees and give the new vertex indegree two. This operation is a structural comparison, not an enumeration rule for all two-outdegree graphs. In particular, it imposes no indegree restriction on the original global search.\par
